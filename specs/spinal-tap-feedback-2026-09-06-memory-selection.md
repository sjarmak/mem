# Feedback: a delivery design can hide an oracle selector

Date: 2026-09-06
Task/artifact: [Memory Beads assessment](memory-beads-critique-2026-09-06.md), [worked interaction](memory-beads-worked-example-2026-09-06.md)
Evidence at capture: untriaged

## Comment or observation

Agent observation, not user feedback: an independent evidence reviewer challenged a proposed matched-content delivery experiment because the favored task-context consumer was implicitly given correct task-to-memory references. Equal record contents did not imply equal relevance information.

No user evaluation of the resulting recommendation has been received.

## Baseline and result

The initial candidate combined task, file, and error cues and proposed comparing identical stored facts across memory interfaces. The reviewer identified two unresolved inputs: correct capture and correct selection. The final recommendation narrows the first test to one task-reference trigger, supplies equivalent selection cues across arms, labels pre-authored links explicitly, and postpones an end-to-end claim until capture and reference creation are measured.

## Evidence and limits

The assessment's next steps and the worked interaction's inspection notes preserve this change. It is a design correction, not an experimental result or evidence of creative value. The [existing cue-delivery paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20972v1) also prevented presenting an existing mechanism as a new architecture. The feedback packet is retained locally to keep the detailed task context with its evidence.

## Proposed lesson and closure check

On a later skill-maintenance pass, consider whether reviewer briefs for architecture experiments should explicitly ask who supplies the inputs that make the proposed mechanism succeed. Test the prompt on a different design with an implicit perfect selector; retain the lesson only if it exposes a real confound without adding generic checklist overhead.

This is evidence for later maintenance, not an instruction change.
